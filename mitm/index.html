Man in the Middle Attack

The attacker can secretly intercept messages between 2 or more parties who believe they are communicating with each other. Eavesdropping and traffic padding are examples.

👂 Eavesdropping

The attacker listens to the messages passing between the parties without their knowledge.

📦 Traffic padding

Listed in the notes as an example of this class of attack on communication between the parties.

    How This Vulnerability Is Prevented

    The modified message sent by the attacker becomes the main mode of communication, which is the wrong message. This vulnerability can be overcome by using digital signatures and public-key certificates.

    🔏 Digital signatures

    Sender A signs the public key A1 with her private signing key. Receiver B checks the signature before using it. Attacker D cannot produce a valid signature for D3 in A's name.

    📜 Public-key certificates

    A trusted certificate authority signs a certificate that ties a public key to an identity. B accepts a key only if its certificate is valid, and D has no certificate that says D3 belongs to A.

    Why the attack fails with them

    1. Sender A sends A1 together with her signature or certificate.
    2. Attacker D replaces it with D3, but cannot sign D3 as A.
    3. Receiver B verifies the signature or certificate and the check fails.
    4. B rejects D3, so no key K1 is ever shared with the attacker. The same check protects B1 on the way back to A.
